# Research basis (summary)

The full cited write-up is the Claude doc "Abstract Reasoning Trainer — Research Basis": https://claude.ai/artifact/MQS7iskYwuW7oCy51JsRwB

## One correct answer
- The test-development standard is one keyed answer per item. Item analysis flags any distractor that attracts high scorers, as a sign of ambiguity or a wrong key.
- Ambiguity is logically possible, because any finite set of panels fits more than one rule. Tests resolve it with a parsimony convention, options built so only one fits, and piloting. Researchers have built deliberately ambiguous Raven-like items (Kirby & Lawson, 1983, cited in Kunda et al., 2016). Machine-generated sets such as RAVEN and I-RAVEN contain accidental ones (Xu et al., 2023).
- On the original RAVEN set, models that saw only the answer options scored 70–90%+, because each distractor changed one attribute of the answer. That is the reason for invariant 3 in CLAUDE.md.

## Difficulty and rules
- Carpenter, Just & Shell (1990): constant in a row, pairwise progression, distribution of three, addition/subtraction, and distribution of two (XOR) explain almost all APM items.
- Difficulty drivers: number of rules and how abstract they are (goal management / working memory), plus element layout (correspondence finding). See Embretson's cognitive design system and J. Intelligence 2025, 13(2), 22.

## Distractors
- Becker et al. (2016): with no options to choose from, the matrices correlated 0.54 with a standard intelligence battery. Perceptual distractors gave 0.38 and conceptual distractors 0.12. Response elimination weakens validity, so the app teaches constructive matching.

## Practice effects
- Hausknecht et al. (2007): d = 0.26 overall, larger with coaching. Scharfen et al. (2018): gains plateau after the third test. Hayes et al. (2015): a third of the gain is strategy change.
